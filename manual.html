TradeBuilt Electrical Schematic Simulator

User Manual

An interactive electrical schematic and circuit simulator for students, apprentices, instructors, and skilled trades professionals. Design, wire, energize, and troubleshoot both AC and DC circuits with real-time Ohm's Law calculations, electron flow animation, and IEEE/ANSI schematic symbols. HVAC/R parts such as compressors and contactors are included as practical skilled-trades examples — the simulator itself is not limited to HVAC work.

Created and Designed by Kenneth J. Thompson

Getting Started

Open index.html in any modern web browser (Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge). No installation, server, or internet connection required — everything runs locally in the browser.

The top toolbar contains all primary controls for the simulator.

Editor Tools

Located in the left sidebar panel.

Select Tool

  • Click a component or wire to select it
  • Drag selected items to reposition them on the grid
  • Right-click for a context menu with Delete, Properties, and Rotate options
  • Click and drag on empty space to box-select multiple items
  • Drag any item in a multi-selection to move the entire group

Wire Tool

  • Click a grid point to start a wire, then click a second grid point to complete it
  • Wires snap to the grid at full and half-grid intervals
  • Wires automatically create junction dots where 3 or more connections meet
  • Wire color can be changed via the Properties panel (Black, Red, Green, Light Grey)

Comment Box Tool

  • Click and drag to draw a rectangular comment region on the canvas
  • Type text inline with a live preview
  • Choose from 7 border colors: Black, Grey, Blue, Green, Yellow, Red, Orange
  • Double-click an existing comment box to edit its text
  • Resize by dragging any corner or edge handle
  • Comments can be selected, moved, and deleted like other items

Components

Power

120V Power Source

  • Adjustable voltage (default 120V) and frequency (default 60 Hz)
  • Terminals labeled L1 (hot) and N (neutral)
  • Can be toggled on/off via the Properties panel
  • Right-click and select "Flip L1 / N" to swap terminal orientation
  • Detects short circuits and overload conditions

240V Power Source — Single phase, two hot legs labeled L1 and L2. Same properties as the 120V source.

480V Δ Power Source — Three phase delta, three terminals labeled L1, L2 and L3, with no neutral. 480V line-to-line.

480V Wye Power Source — Three phase wye (480Y/277V): three hot legs plus a neutral, so line-to-neutral measures 277V.

DC Battery — Direct current source, default 12V, terminals labeled + and −. Flip with F or the right-click menu.

Earth Ground — Ties a node to earth potential as a 0V reference.

Transformer

  • Steps voltage up or down between primary and secondary windings
  • Default: 120V primary / 24V secondary
  • 4-terminal component with L1, N (primary) and R, C (secondary) labels
  • Displays separate PRI and SEC data boxes with independent voltage, current, and wattage readings
  • Power is conserved across windings

Loads

Resistor — Adjustable resistance in ohms (default 100 ohm). Drawn with IEC rectangle symbol and color band indicators.

Light Bulb — Default 100W / 144 ohm. Glows when energized with a gentle pulse animation. Bulb color options: Yellow (default), Red, Blue, Orange.

Fan Motor — Default 1/2 HP at 120V, 300W, 38.6 ohm winding. Winding resistance is derived from the selected horsepower and motor voltage, and the motor is modelled as an inductive load (default power factor 0.75), so running current follows the impedance rather than resistance alone. Blades spin when energized — speed scales with power delivered. Animated wind swoosh arcs shown during operation.

Compressor — Three-terminal CSR motor (Common, Start, Run) with separate run and start windings (defaults 1.5 ohm run / 2.5 ohm start) and an optional start-winding cutout after a set time.

Capacitor — Adjustable capacitance in microfarads (default 10 uF). Reactance (Xc) calculated automatically. Supports fault modes (open/short).

Outlet — Configurable wattage load (default 0W = empty outlet). Shows live voltage and power consumption when energized. Supports fault modes (open/short).

Switches

Single Pole Switch — Click to toggle between open and closed states.

DPDT Switch — Places two switch poles sharing a switch group, so both throw together.

Time Delay Switch — Starts a countdown when electricity reaches its terminals. Closes automatically after the set delay (default 5 seconds). Displays a countdown timer with a clock icon and progress arc.

Controls

Single Pole Contactor — Places a coil and contact pair linked by a shared group letter. The coil (default 24V, 200 ohm) goes in the control circuit, the contact goes in the power circuit. When the coil is energized, all contacts in the same group close automatically. Multiple contacts can share one coil for multi-pole control.

Relay — Same coil-and-contact pairing as the contactor, at a lower rating (default 24V, 150 ohm coil). Contacts can be set normally open (NO) or normally closed (NC) from the Properties panel.

Protection

High Voltage Fuse — Default rated at 15A. Blows when current exceeds rated amps, breaking the circuit.

Low Voltage Fuse — Default rated at 3A. Same behavior as the HV fuse, designed for low voltage circuits.

Time Delay Fuse — Default rated at 30A. Tolerates an overload for its set delay (default 10 seconds) before blowing, so motor inrush does not nuisance-trip it.

Single Pole Breaker — Default rated at 20A. Trips when current exceeds rated amps. Reset via the Properties panel or the Reset toolbar button.

Technician's Tool Bag

The tool bag sits at the bottom of the sidebar. Hover over the bag image to reveal four diagnostic instruments. Click an instrument to open it.

Digital Multimeter

A full-featured digital multimeter with five measurement modes.

ModeSymbolDescription
VACV ACMeasures AC voltage between two probe points
VDCV DCMeasures DC voltage between two probe points
AACA AmpsMeasures AC current through a component (place probes across it)
OhmsΩMeasures resistance between two points (circuit must be de-energized)
CapµFMeasures capacitance in microfarads (circuit must be de-energized)

How to use:

  1. Click the Digital Multimeter button in the tool bag to open it
  2. Select a measurement mode using the buttons on the meter face
  3. Drag the red and black probe tips from the meter jacks onto circuit nodes
  4. The meter display updates in real-time with the measured value
  5. Drag probes back to the jacks or close the meter when finished

Ohmmeter Charge Animation: When measuring a capacitor with the ohmmeter, the display simulates a real charging curve — resistance climbs from near zero up to O.L. (open line) over time. Larger capacitors charge more slowly, just like a real meter.

Non-Contact Voltage (NCV) Tester

Detects the presence of AC voltage without touching bare conductors.

RangeIndication
12V – 90VLow range — green LED
90V – 1000VHigh range — red LED
No voltageNo indication

How to use:

  1. Click the NCV Tester button in the tool bag to open it
  2. Drag the probe tip near any wire or component on the canvas
  3. The LED bar and status text update based on detected voltage
  4. The tester detects voltage within approximately 1.5 grid cells of the tip
  5. Drag the tip back to the tester body or close it when finished

Amp Clamp (Clamp Meter)

Measures AC current by clamping around a wire — no need to break the circuit.

  1. Click the Amp Clamp button in the tool bag to open it
  2. Drag the jaw icon over any energized wire on the canvas
  3. The display shows the current flowing through that wire in amps
  4. The jaw detects current within approximately 1.2 grid cells

Clipboard (Technician's Notes)

A multi-page notepad for writing observations, procedures, or troubleshooting notes during a session.

  • Add unlimited pages with the "+ Add Page" button
  • Navigate between pages with the arrow buttons
  • Delete pages with the X button on each page
  • All notes persist in browser storage across page refreshes
  • Drag the clipboard by its top handle to reposition
  • Resize by dragging any corner or edge (minimum 200 x 260 pixels)

Circuit Logic

Ohm's Law

All circuit values are calculated in real-time using nodal analysis:

  • V = I × R (Voltage = Current × Resistance)
  • P = I × V (Power = Current × Voltage)

Series Circuits

Components wired in a single path share the same current. Voltage divides proportionally across each component based on its resistance.

Parallel Circuits

Components wired side by side share the same voltage. Current divides between branches inversely proportional to their resistance.

Short Circuit Detection

If a zero-resistance path connects the source terminals directly, the source displays a SHORT CIRCUIT warning. Fuses and breakers in the path will trip or blow before the warning appears if they are rated below the fault current.

Fuse and Breaker Protection

When current through a fuse or breaker exceeds its rated amperage, the device automatically triggers — fuses blow and breakers trip — breaking the circuit to protect downstream components.

Transformer Voltage Stepping

Secondary voltage is calculated as: Primary Voltage × (Secondary Rating / Primary Rating). Power is conserved across windings so secondary current adjusts accordingly.

Contactor Control

When a contactor coil receives sufficient voltage, all contacts sharing the same group letter close simultaneously. This allows a low-voltage control circuit (e.g., 24V thermostat) to switch a high-voltage power circuit (e.g., 240V compressor).

Electron Flow Animation

  • Animated dots flow along energized wires showing current direction
  • Flow direction follows voltage potential from L1 (hot) toward N (neutral)
  • Dot speed scales with current magnitude — higher current means faster flow
  • Electrons use AC oscillation synchronized across the entire circuit
  • Open switches and blown fuses correctly stop flow on the broken side
  • Open time delay switches show electrons flowing up to their terminals (voltage presence without current)

Properties Panel

Right-click any component or wire and select Properties to open the editor.

  • Drag the panel by its title bar to reposition
  • A dashed tether line connects the panel to the selected item
  • Editable fields vary by component type: Voltage, resistance, watt rating, capacitance, delay time, coil voltage, contactor group letter, bulb color, fuse/breaker rated amps, wire color, fault mode, and component label
  • Click Close to dismiss or Delete to remove the component

Fault Simulation

Fault modes allow instructors and students to simulate real-world component failures for troubleshooting practice.

Components that support fault modes: Resistor, Light Bulb, Fan, Capacitor, Contactor Coil, Outlet

FaultBehavior
NoneNormal operation
Open CircuitComponent acts as if physically broken — no current flows through it
Short CircuitComponent is bypassed with zero resistance — current flows through without voltage drop

Set fault modes in the Properties panel. When the Faults view toggle is enabled, a label appears below the faulted component indicating the fault type.

Saving and Loading

Save

  1. Click Settings > Save Project in the toolbar
  2. Enter a name for the project
  3. Click Save New — the project is saved to your TradeBuilt account
  4. To replace an existing project instead, click Overwrite on its row and confirm

Two projects are allowed to have the same name. They stay separate projects, and the dialog tells you when a name is already in use so you can pick a clearer one.

Load

  1. Click Settings > Load Project in the toolbar
  2. Browse your saved projects, newest first, showing component count, wire count, and when each was last saved
  3. Click a project to load it
  4. Click Rename to change a project's name, or the × button to delete it

Auto-Save

The circuit you are working on auto-saves on every change and restores automatically when the page is refreshed. If the circuit was energized when you closed the browser, it will restore in the energized state. This is separate from your saved projects — it is a safety net for the work in progress, kept on the device you are using.

Where saved projects live

Saved projects are stored with your TradeBuilt account, not on one particular device. Sign in on another computer or iPad and your projects are there. Renaming or deleting a project changes it everywhere you are signed in.

You only ever see your own projects. Signing out clears them from the screen, so a second person signing in on the same device sees their own projects and none of yours.

The auto-save is the exception: it stays in the browser on the device you are using, and it is not permanent. Clearing your browser's cookies and site data deletes it, and on iPad and Mac, Safari's “Clear History and Website Data” does the same. Safari can also discard it on its own if you do not visit the simulator for about a week. Save your work as a project so it is kept with your account rather than relying on the auto-save.

If your connection drops, you can keep working. Saves made while offline are held and sent as soon as you are back online.

Export is still the way to hand a circuit to somebody else, or to keep a copy that does not depend on an account — see below.

Export Schematic to File

Exporting writes a project to a .tbss.json file you keep — a backup that survives clearing your browser, and the way to move a circuit to another device or hand it to someone else.

  1. Click Settings > Export Schematic to File, name the file, and it downloads to your device

Export always writes the circuit currently on the canvas. To export a saved project, load it first, then export.

The file holds the whole project: components, wires, comments, camera position, view options, your clipboard notes, and the position and mode of the multimeter, amp clamp, and NCV tester.

Import Schematic from File

  1. Click Settings > Import Schematic from File and choose a .tbss.json file
  2. The project is added to your saved projects list, which opens so you can click it to load

Importing does not disturb the circuit currently on the canvas. If a saved project already uses the same name, you are asked whether to overwrite it or import a copy.
